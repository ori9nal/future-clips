How the phone was made

A screenshot pasted flat onto a photo reads as a sticker, because a phone in the hand is a plane under perspective. These three files are the whole trick.

01 — Drawn

The app screen

Rendered at 900×1950 from the same font files as the slides. The accent is sampled from the rim light in the photograph, so the glow on the glass and the glow in the room match.

02 — Generated

The photograph

Generated with a deliberately blank screen. Two other attempts were rejected — one turned the phone into a glowing egg, the other had no phone at all.

03 — Composited

The composite

The UI is mapped through the homography taking the unit square to the screen's four corners, then the photo's own specular highlight is put back on top — neutral, and damped where the pixel is saturated, or the original screen bleeds through as a coloured smear.

What the build refuses to ship

Each of these exists because it caught something in this set.

  • Font preflightRasterise a probe in every family and compare its ink width to the loaded TTF. Catches silent fallback — which had been rendering headlines 40% wider than the fitter allowed.
  • Fit contractEvery headline and paragraph is sized by binary search against real advance widths and kern pairs. Copy that will not fit throws instead of overflowing.
  • Overflow assertionStacked blocks assert their final baseline clears the page furniture. Caught the results slide sitting 10px under its own page number.
  • Derived figuresChart geometry and the numbers in the copy read from the same arrays, so a headline cannot drift from the grid beneath it.
  • Logic checkGap-closing hooks, cross-slide numeric contradictions, banned claims, missing CTA verb, missing required mention. Free, deterministic, runs before any paid call.

Where this is honest about its limits. The imagery comes from a keyless free endpoint, which cannot hold a character across generations — so there is no recurring mascot and no person in frame. That is the ceiling on this set, not a stylistic choice.

The vision judge is written and wired but unrun: it needs an Anthropic key. The deterministic half of it — the logic checks above — runs free and passes.
