## The product

**Vokabo Core 800** is a mobile app (iOS + Android) built on one idea: about 800 words cover roughly 75% of everyday spoken conversation, and most courses spend six months on the other 25%. The app teaches only that core list — no chapters on stationery, no "the elephant is on the table." You pick a language (Spanish, French, Italian, German, Portuguese, Japanese), and Vokabo Core 800 drills the list through 90-second spoken rounds: it says a phrase, you say it back, the mic scores your timing and gives you the fix. There's a progress ring that shows what percentage of normal conversation you can now follow, which is the screen people screenshot. Free tier gives 120 words; the full 800 is £6.99/month.

## Hard rules — these get a post rejected

Rejected means unpaid, and views on a rejected post don't roll into your total.

**Banned claims.** Do not say, imply, caption or text-overlay any of the following:

- "Fluent in 30 days," "fluent in three months," or any fluency-by-date promise
- "Learn Spanish in a week" / any total-mastery-by-timeframe framing
- "Scientifically proven," "neuroscience-backed," "clinically tested," "research shows you only need 800 words." The 800-word figure comes from published word-frequency corpora and you may say *"frequency lists suggest around 800 words cover most everyday conversation"* — you may not upgrade that into proof language.
- Anything about passing a specific exam (DELE, DELF, JLPT, Goethe) or meeting a visa, citizenship or immigration language requirement
- "Replaces a tutor," "replaces classes," "you'll never need a teacher"
- Claims about brain health, memory improvement, dementia prevention, or cognitive benefits of any kind
- Guaranteed refunds, "free forever," or any pricing not on the current store listing

**Also rejected:**

- Naming competitors. No "better than [green owl app]," no visual parody of a named competitor's mascot, no side-by-side with another app's UI.
- Fake accents or ethnic caricature. Do not do a "native speaker" bit in a voice that isn't yours.
- AI voice cloning of real people, living or dead. AI narration of your own script in a generic TTS voice is allowed and must be disclosed in the caption.
- Fabricated user quotes or fake DM screenshots.
- Filming a real stranger's face without consent (café staff, waiters, cab drivers — a very tempting angle here, and no).
- Undisclosed partnership. Every post carries **#ad** or the platform's paid-partnership label in the first line of the caption.
- Copyrighted music outside the platform commercial library.
- Editing the progress-ring percentage or any other in-app number.

## Slide count and format

Five to nine slides. **Six and seven are the sweet spot** — everything we ran under five slides this cycle underperformed on watch-through, and nines only hold up if slides 4–7 are genuinely doing something.

Target 22–38 seconds. Hook in the first 1.5 seconds, spoken *and* on screen. Vertical 9:16, burned-in captions, no letterboxing.

What performs: **the moment of failure, then the mechanism.** Our top three clips last cycle all opened with someone who had studied a language and still couldn't function in it, then explained the frequency-list idea in one sentence, then showed the drill. Explaining the app first kills it every time.

## Angles that work

- **"Four years of Spanish and I couldn't order a coffee."** School/college study versus one real interaction. Highest-saving angle we have.
- **The 800 list reveal.** Read the first fifteen words out loud on screen and let people realise how boring — and how obviously correct — they are.
- **Textbook vs. street.** A phrase from a course book, then what people actually say instead. Works especially well for Japanese and French.
- **The percentage ring as a scoreboard.** Day 1 versus day 11. Show real numbers from your own account.
- **"I tested it on my [flatmate / partner / coworker] who actually speaks it."** Consent on camera, please.
- **Trip countdown.** Nine days before a holiday, what's the highest-leverage thing to study. No promises about how the trip goes.

Avoid: generic "5 apps you need" listicles, AI slideshow travel b-roll with a robot voice, and anything framed as language-learning advice for exams or immigration.
