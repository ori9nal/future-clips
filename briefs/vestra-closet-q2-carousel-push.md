## The Product

Vestra Closet is a resale app for second-hand designer and premium high-street clothing. You photograph a piece, the app prices it against completed sales in its own catalogue, and it lists it across the marketplace in about ninety seconds. Buyers get a delivery-tracked item that's been through Vestra's in-house check desk in Leicester before it ships; sellers get paid out to bank or store credit once the buyer accepts. The pitch to the user is blunt: you already own £400 of clothes you don't wear, and you're still spending £30 a week on things that pill after two washes.

## Platform, Language, Audience

- **Platforms:** TikTok photo carousels and Instagram carousels. Nothing else. No Reels, no YouTube Shorts, no Pinterest.
- **Language:** English. UK-native phrasing preferred — "trainers", "jumper", "quid". American slang reads as imported and underperforms in this account's data.
- **Audience:** 18–32, UK and Ireland weighted. Your account should already skew female 60%+ or be fashion/thrift-coded. General meme accounts, motivation accounts and repost pages are not eligible.
- **Account minimum:** 4,000 followers and at least three posts in the last 30 days that cleared 10,000 views organically.
- Posts must be original carousels made for this brief. Reused decks from other resale clients will be caught and rejected.

## Hard Rules — Rejection List

A submission is rejected outright, with no partial payment, if it does any of the following:

- Claims or implies **guaranteed authenticity**. Do not write "100% authenticated", "verified real", "every item is genuine" or "authenticity guaranteed". Vestra Closet operates a check desk; it does not issue authentication guarantees, and this wording has caused the client problems before.
- Makes **earnings promises**. No "make £500 a month flipping", no "I made rent selling clothes", no income screenshots, no "side hustle that pays your bills". You may say what *you personally* listed and what it sold for, framed as one sale, not as a repeatable income.
- Uses **absolute sustainability claims** — "carbon neutral", "zero waste", "eco-friendly", "saves the planet". "Second-hand", "resale", "circular" and "one less new thing bought" are all fine.
- Names a **real retailer, brand or influencer in a negative frame**. You cannot say a named fast-fashion chain is exploitative, low quality, or a scam. Talk about your own spending, not other companies' behaviour.
- Uses the words **"dupe", "replica", "inspired by"** or shows anything that could read as counterfeit.
- Shows **prices for items not currently live** in the app, or invented sale figures.
- Uses body-shaming, "before I lost weight", or any framing that ties the wardrobe clear-out to a body change.
- Uses licensed music on the Instagram cut, or any audio the client can't clear commercially.
- Runs without **#ad** in the first line of the caption. Not buried, not after the hashtag block.

## Placement Rule

The app interface must be visible in-frame. Screen recordings, screenshots and photographed handheld phone shots all count, as long as the Vestra Closet listing screen or home feed is legible.

| Carousel length | App must appear by |
|---|---|
| 5–6 slides | Slide 3 |
| 7–8 slides | Slide 4 |
| 9–10 slides | Slide 5 |

The final slide must always carry the app name and a plain call to action. If the app appears only on the last slide, the post fails placement and is not paid.

## Length, Format, Angles

Run **6 to 9 slides**. Six is the floor; below that the swipe-through rate collapses and the view count follows it. Nine is the ceiling before drop-off eats the payout.

What performs on this account's historic data: slide one is a photograph, not a text card. A messy floordrobe, an overstuffed rail, a bin bag by the door, a receipt. The text overlay on slide one should be a confession, not a claim — "I counted what I spent on clothes last year and I want to be sick" outperformed every polished hook we tested by roughly 3x. Keep overlay text under twelve words a slide.

**Angles that work:**

1. **The audit.** You add up what you spent in a year. You don't editorialise. You just list it, slide by slide, and then show the first three things you listed on Vestra Closet.
2. **One piece, its whole life.** A coat you bought new, wore twice, listed, and what it sold for. Honest and small. This is the highest-converting angle we have.
3. **The rail swap.** Before and after a clear-out — same rail, fewer things, better things. Photograph it properly, in daylight.
4. **"I stopped buying new for 60 days."** Diary format. What you listed, what you bought second-hand instead, what you actually missed.
5. **Listing speed.** Literal screen-record of one item going from photo to live listing, timed. Works because nobody believes it's ninety seconds.

Do not do haul content. Do not do "get ready with me". Both were tested and both flopped for this brand.
