## The product

Loadout Gym App is a workout builder that starts from an equipment list instead of a template. You tell it what your gym actually has — one squat rack, no leg press, three pairs of dumbbells that stop at 30kg, a cable stack that's always occupied — and it writes the session around those constraints, swapping lifts automatically when a machine is missing or taken. It stores a saved "loadout" per gym, so the hotel gym on a work trip gets a different plan than your usual place, and it tracks sets and loads across both. Free tier gives you one saved gym and a rolling 7-day plan; Loadout Plus (£6.99/mo) unlocks unlimited gyms, progression tracking and the "busy gym" mode that reroutes your session in real time when a station is full.

## Hard rules — these get a post rejected

- **No body-composition promises.** No "lose 10lb in 6 weeks", no "get shredded by summer", no before/after weight or measurement claims tied to using the app.
- **No health, medical or rehab framing.** Nothing about fixing injuries, back pain, joint issues, posture correction, hormones, testosterone, or training around a condition. If you have an injury, don't mention it in the clip.
- **No "clinically proven", "science-backed", "physio-approved", "certified by trainers"** or similar authority claims. Loadout Gym App is a planning tool, not a credentialled professional.
- **No nutrition or supplement talk.** No calorie targets, no macros, no protein recommendations, no PEDs or "natty" discourse.
- **No fake screens.** The app footage must be the real app on your real phone. No mockups, no Figma, no screen-recordings borrowed from another clipper.
- **No invented pricing or features.** Free tier and £6.99/mo Plus only. Do not say "free forever", do not say a Plus feature is free.
- **No shirtless-in-the-mirror openers**, no gym-creep or filming other members' faces, no unlicensed music beyond the platform library, no other fitness app named or shown on screen.
- **Disclosure:** paid partnership toggle on for both platforms, plus `#ad` in the first line of the caption.

## Placement rule (app must be shown)

The build screen is the whole point — if a viewer never sees it, the post doesn't count. Follow the slide count you're actually posting:

| Slide count | Where the app screen must appear |
|---|---|
| 3–4 slides | Slide 2 — the equipment-select screen, legible, held ≥1.5s |
| 5–6 slides | Slide 3 and again on the final slide |
| 7–8 slides | Slide 2, slide 5, and the final slide |
| Single-shot video (no slides) | Within the first 5 seconds, on screen for a cumulative 4s minimum |

Legible means: no speed-ramp over the screen, no heavy filter, no text sticker covering the equipment list. Vertical 9:16, 1080×1920 minimum.

## Slide count and what performs

Five to six slides is the sweet spot. Three feels like an ad; nine loses people before the payoff. The strongest structure we're seeing is **problem → attempt → app → session → result**, with the problem stated in text on slide one before anyone speaks.

Openers that are working: the squat rack occupied by someone doing bicep curls; dumbbells that stop at 30kg; a hotel gym that is two treadmills and a resistance band; "my gym has no leg press and I've been avoiding legs for a month". Hook text should name the constraint, not the app.

Angles the client wants:

- **"My gym is trash and I made it work."** Pan the actual gym, show what's missing, show the loadout it built anyway.
- **Travel / hotel gym.** Two saved gyms, wildly different plans, same tracking.
- **Busy-hour reroute.** Station's taken, app swaps the lift, you keep moving. This is the highest-converting angle so far.
- **Beginner walkthrough.** "I didn't know what to do in here" → equipment list → a session with an order to it. Keep it descriptive, not instructional — show what the app gave you, don't coach the viewer.
- **The switch.** You used to screenshot a programme that assumed a full commercial gym. Show why that broke.

Avoid: chasing trending audio at the expense of the app screen, "day in my life" formats where the product lands at second 40, and anything that reads as a gym-shaming video about a real identifiable person.
